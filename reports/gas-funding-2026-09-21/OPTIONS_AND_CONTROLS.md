# Gas funding options and security controls

Study date: **21 September 2026**. Code and policy inspected in `product` at commit `60c946519fcc582a9fa32713b2372ee47f455ade`. This is an architecture study, not an activated funding policy, implementation, wallet authorization, or transaction. No key was loaded and no transaction was sent. Monetary gas scenarios are supplied separately by the gas-cost study; this report identifies the costs each scenario must include.

**Recommendation:** begin with A, a small project-funded ETH balance in each execution role and a manually replenished treasury. Add B, bounded conversion of already-collected company revenue, when actual fee receipts and qualified conversion routes justify its operating cost. Keep A as B's emergency fallback. C, sponsorship, is technically available on Robinhood mainnet and merits a separate integration if removing treasury gas management justifies the provider and engineering costs. Per-user gas escrow is a different commercial product requiring an explicit policy change; it is not a funding shortcut under the current all-in 2% tariff.

## What the existing implementation permits

Robinhood mainnet is chain **4663**, with **ETH as its native gas token**. An ERC-20 balance, including WETH, does not by itself fund an ordinary operator transaction. [Robinhood network configuration](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/connecting/).

The approved [pricing policy](../../PRICING_POLICY.md) charges 2% of consumed earned fees, once, with operator compensation and gas inside that cut. The [reinvestment policy](../../REINVESTMENT_POLICY.md) forbids principal withdrawal, new wallet pulls and a fixed idle reserve of user fees. Its 15% gas margin is an estimation buffer, not an additional customer charge or a share of user assets held back. A project treasury may hold its **already-paid revenue** as ETH working capital; that is distinct from retaining extra fees inside owners' vaults.

## Comparison

| Option | Execution and payer | Fit with current contracts and tariff | Main advantage | Main drawback and added cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Prefunded ETH with manual refill** | Project treasury sends ETH separately to operator and price publisher before work. Each EOA pays its own transactions. | Direct fit; no vault or tariff change. Monitoring/runbook still needed. | Fewest new dependencies; predictable recovery when fee tokens cannot be sold. | Human refill delay and ETH working capital; funding transactions, custody operations and staff time. |
| **B. Automatic company-revenue conversion/refill** | After confirmed 2% receipts: retain native ETH, unwrap qualified WETH, or sell approved company tokens into ETH; top up the role below its threshold. | Fits the tariff if only company assets are spent. Separate service or controller; no need to weaken vault accounting. | Less routine manual work and automatic conversion of earned revenue to operating liquidity. | More hot signing authority, conversion gas, pool fees, price impact, failed conversions and route/issuer risk. Bootstrap ETH is still necessary. |
| **C. Smart-account/paymaster sponsorship** | Smart-account calls are funded by a provider's sponsorship facility or a company-funded paymaster. | Chain support exists; Domino's EOA profiles, signer and receipt handling need a new reviewed integration. Company sponsorship preserves the tariff. | Provider billing can remove hot-key ETH refill work; account modules can bound signing powers. | Provider/EntryPoint/account dependencies, validation overhead, billing limits and fees; does not remove the underlying gas cost. |

### A. Prefunded ETH treasury

Keep long-term company funds under separate custody. Fund small, explicit ETH targets for the operator and price publisher. The treasury/refill signer also needs ETH to send top-ups. Keep the guardian independently recoverable, with enough access to gas for its emergency functions. Receipt accounting attributes execution, publication and refill expenses to Domino rather than charging the owner's remaining 98% again.

A is appropriate for low initial volume: fees can accumulate as tokens while the project supplies working capital. The treasury can later sell them in an approved manual batch. Revenue and funding must remain separate in the books: a top-up is not new service revenue, and a valuation of illiquid token fees is not spendable ETH.

The operational failure is missed replenishment. Check balances and committed pending costs continuously, alert before the next refill window, and stop scheduling new operator work before a key is unable to complete it. One large treasury balance does not help an empty publisher account. Publication may spend gas even when no subsequent compound succeeds or becomes economical.

### B. Convert already-collected revenue and refill automatically

Use a durable treasury job with a narrow command set: transfer ETH to an approved role; unwrap a fixed amount of an admitted wrapped-native token; or sell an exact maximum amount of a specific treasury token along an admitted route. Reconstruct calldata inside its signer from validated fields, as the current executor does for its own domain. Do not give the execution operator the treasury's unrestricted signer.

The three asset paths are different:

1. **Native ETH revenue:** top up directly, retaining gas for the top-up transaction and emergency reserve.
2. **Qualified wrapped ETH revenue:** unwrap rather than pay DEX trading fees. The previously reviewed Robinhood WETH proxy is `0x0bd7d308f8e1639fab988df18a8011f41eacad73`. Its reviewed aeWETH implementation, `0xc6b81b429797e0f555440b70cd99e032d7ae947e`, exposes `withdraw(uint256)` and `withdrawTo(address,uint256)`. The verified source burns the caller's units and sends the same quantity of native ETH; this path has no DEX slippage in that implementation. `withdrawTo` could combine unwrapping and funding one approved key. [Verified implementation source, `aeWETH.sol:54–65`](https://sourcify.dev/server/v2/contract/4663/0xc6b81b429797e0f555440b70cd99e032d7ae947e?fields=abi,sources).
3. **Other token revenue:** obtain a realizable quote, then swap into native ETH or qualified WETH followed by unwrap. A pair that earns neither native ETH nor WETH requires this path. A token symbol or nominal USD valuation proves neither a viable route nor enough liquidity. If conversion is unavailable, the revenue remains a token asset and A supplies gas or automation waits.

The unwrap observation is a **source-level candidate**, not present-day execution qualification. The earlier [token review](../UPGRADEABLE_TOKEN_REVIEW.md) qualified ordinary token transfers and approvals; it did not qualify the unwrap path. The token is upgradeable. Recheck the proxy implementation, source-derived authority/backing conditions, and actual treasury account behavior at a fresh block; simulate and fork-test exact token burn and native receipt. Do not equate same-chain WETH unwrapping with withdrawing through the Ethereum bridge. Robinhood documents a roughly seven-day canonical L2-to-L1 challenge window, a different operation. [Robinhood bridging](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/bridging/).

Follow-up cost sampling in this study executed `withdraw(uint256)` and a separate
native transfer on an isolated fork of the project node: 47,873 and 21,000 child
gas respectively. [Measured transaction footprints](gas-measurements-complete.json).
This supports the cost model for that two-call sequence; `withdrawTo` was not
executed, and the sample does not replace proxy/custody qualification for a live
treasury or imply that any mainnet transfer occurred.

Bootstrap cannot depend on future fees. An EOA holding only WETH or stablecoins has no ETH to initiate its own unwrap/swap; the first compound and publication must also happen before their potential service revenue is available. Seed the treasury/refill signer, operator and publisher independently, retain an emergency ETH floor, and refill before that floor is reached. A provider sponsor can front this expense only after a separate option-C integration.

Batch conversion by an economic threshold. Required costs include approval or permit setup, swap/unwrap gas, token transfer/top-up gas, pool charges, price impact, failed/replaced attempts, and the applicable infrastructure charge. A `minOut` does not make a small conversion economical. Execute only if conservative net ETH received exceeds the conversion cost and selected margin, except for an explicit emergency subsidy budget. Do not use the 2% of an unexecuted future job as collateral.

If an existing recipient is an EOA, a different refiller cannot spend its tokens without authorization. Choose between a separately restricted treasury signing service and a narrowly scoped allowance/controller. Software signing rules do not constrain an attacker who steals the unrestricted EOA key; keep hot holdings small, or use independently enforced contract custody limits. Granting an unlimited allowance merely moves unrestricted treasury risk to the spender. An EOA's native ETH cannot be moved by an ERC-20 allowance. If the recipient is a contract wallet, qualify its module/authorization and passive receipt behavior; do not assume an arbitrary wallet product/module is already installed or reviewed on 4663. A new treasury controller adds code, audit and deployment work beyond an off-chain refiller.

### C. Sponsorship and the distinct escrow alternative

**Mainnet support is verified in primary documentation.** Robinhood explicitly documents ERC-4337 and EIP-7702 support on chain 4663, EntryPoint versions 0.6/0.7/0.8, and Alchemy/ZeroDev integration examples. Alchemy independently lists Robinhood mainnet and testnet as supported for bundling, gas sponsorship and ERC-20 gas payments. These are provider/network capability statements, not evidence that Domino's provider account, selected account code or production policy has been qualified. [Robinhood account abstraction](https://docs.robinhood.com/chain/account-abstraction/), [Alchemy supported chains](https://www.alchemy.com/docs/wallets/supported-chains).

ERC-4337 bundles signed UserOperations through an EntryPoint. A conventional on-chain paymaster requires a native-token deposit; failed sponsored execution can still consume that deposit. Account/paymaster validation and post-operation work also consume gas. Sponsorship changes who pays and how authorization is handled; it does not make execution free. [ERC-4337 specification](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4337).

There are two treasury arrangements. A self-managed paymaster needs project ETH deposits and still has liquidity/refill obligations. A managed provider can front gas and bill the company in USD, moving the immediate bootstrap requirement from a hot ETH balance to an approved billing facility. Company fee tokens still need realization to pay that bill eventually. [Alchemy sponsorship overview](https://www.alchemy.com/docs/wallets/transactions/sponsor-gas/overview).

As read on 21 September 2026, Alchemy's FAQ publishes an **8% PAYG fee on covered gas**, Enterprise custom pricing, and a **$0/month PAYG mainnet base limit pending custom limits**. Treat those as dated published terms to confirm for the specific account, not an approved credit line. The same FAQ says concurrent processing can briefly exceed configured limits. Keep local atomic budget reservations below the provider cap. [Alchemy Gas Manager FAQ](https://www.alchemy.com/docs/wallets/reference/gas-manager-faqs).

The viable Domino design sponsors company-controlled operator and publisher accounts. A conventional smart account has its own caller address and requires applicable role authorization. EIP-7702 can preserve an existing EOA address, but writes persistent delegation code, incompatible with the present `eth_getCode == 0x` release check. Delegation is not rolled back merely because the transaction's execution fails. Pin reviewed delegate code and initialization, use chain-bound authorizations, restrict session capabilities, and test revocation/recovery separately. These are new authorization and admission requirements, not a reason to remove the EOA check without replacement. [EIP-7702 specification](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-7702).

The runner must verify the UserOperation sender, nonce, EntryPoint/account implementation, exact nested Domino call, success event, transaction inclusion and canonical finality. A successful bundle transaction alone does not prove the particular operation succeeded. Preserve current replay/state/amount limits, replacement recovery and role separation. Qualify publisher sponsorship independently from operator sponsorship; one working sponsored compound does not demonstrate observation availability.

Provider policies can set sender, amount and transaction-count limits and call a webhook for approval. For Domino, the local approver should additionally decode exact approved targets/selectors and nested account calls, reject arbitrary batch additions and fail closed on unavailable policy data. Use separate budgets for operator and publisher and an aggregate project ceiling. [Alchemy policy rules](https://www.alchemy.com/docs/wallets/transactions/sponsor-gas/conditional-sponsorship-rules).

**Per-user gas escrow is a separate variant, not the current recommendation.** If users deposit gas collateral and Domino debits it in addition to the 2% cut, the approved tariff changes. The same is true of an ERC-20 paymaster charging their wallet token balances. It requires explicit product consent, a new accounting contract/policy, quote and debit caps, withdrawal/refund rights, failure-cost rules, isolation between owners and receipts distinguishing actual gas from markup. A refund to a relayer after execution still requires that relayer or sponsor to fund the transaction first. If escrow is funded solely by allocating existing company revenue, it is company sponsorship with an attribution ledger, not a new customer contribution. No variant may silently debit position principal or reinterpret setup consent as ongoing wallet-pull authority.

## Controls shared by the funding options

**Separate custody and permissions.** Use independent treasury, limited refiller, operator, publisher and guardian credentials. An operator compromise must not acquire publisher authority or an unlimited route into company reserves. If one compromised hot key repeatedly drains top-ups, a maximum hot balance alone is insufficient: impose per-top-up, rolling-period and total project limits, refill frequency limits, reconciliation, and an independent stop switch. Avoid a shared unrestricted root credential in the running services. Keep the guardian available if the other services fail.

**Thresholds are per key.** For each role, derive the low-water threshold from its expected transaction rate, a conservative measured all-in transaction cost, refill lead time and retry/emergency allowance. Subtract maximum committed costs of pending transactions from its usable ETH; count mutually exclusive replacements at one nonce by their maximum exposure, not their sum. Set a higher target to avoid tiny repeated top-ups and a strict maximum hot balance. Budget the refiller's own gas separately. There is no defensible universal `0.01 ETH` threshold without workload and chain-cost evidence. If reserves fail, suspend new automated economic work; owner-controlled recovery must remain independent.

**Reconcile actual expense once.** Key funding and reimbursement are company cash movements. Record canonical transaction hash, role, nonce, actual payer, verified fee basis, and the source/amount of top-ups. Include publication even when it earns no fee, mined failures and effective replacements; do not count an unmined dropped replacement as spent gas. Resolve reorgs before settling a claim. Never reimburse an arbitrary caller-provided cost or count both a provider invoice and the same on-chain expense. Allocate shared publisher costs transparently; do not invent a per-vault cost precision the evidence cannot support.

**Only explicit company assets may be sold.** Identify realized revenue by successful receipts and reconcile treasury balances; exclude owners' retained fees, reserves and principal. An allowed company subsidy or other revenue line must be separately budgeted. The assisted-swap charge is separate revenue with separate economics and cannot be assumed to recur with automated compound volume. Company revenue can finance company operations across customers after it has legitimately been earned; that does not authorize taking one owner's assets to execute for another.

**Bound every conversion.** Bind chain 4663, treasury source, exact token address, maximum debit, output asset, destination, route/router identity, minimum output, maximum impact, deadline and gas ceiling. Prefer exact short-lived approvals; clear residual approvals where necessary, and account for that gas. No arbitrary target/calldata, delegatecall, surprise hook, unlimited permit, or user-vault allowance belongs in the refiller. Reconcile net native received and inventory after execution. Contract controllers need reentrancy protection and checks/effects/interactions around token, router, unwrap and recipient calls.

**Treat tokens and market execution as hostile inputs.** Upgradeable or restricted tokens can freeze a treasury/router, alter transfer behavior or invalidate a quote between preparation and inclusion. The existing [WETH/USDG review](../UPGRADEABLE_TOKEN_REVIEW.md) does not qualify a new treasury address or new swap path. Reject unexpected transfer deltas, taxes, rebases and unreviewed callbacks rather than broadening economic transfer tolerances. Do not sell merely because a provider supplies a symbol/price. Use independent price checks, bounded depth/impact, short deadlines and conservative minimum output; no claim of MEV-free execution follows from sequencer ordering. A conversion failure spends company gas and must not block owner recovery or force liquidation of user assets.

## Engineering effort and decision gates

These are **engineering judgment**, expressed as focused engineer-days for an experienced contributor familiar with this repository. They assume working infrastructure access and exclude external audit/provider onboarding waiting time. They are planning ranges, not quotes or verified labor costs. Multiply days by 8 and the team's actual hourly rate for a labor budget; no market labor rate is assumed here.

| Deliverable | Indicative effort | Evidence before enabling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: per-key monitoring, manual refill runbook, budgets and reconciliation | **2–4 days** | Pending/replacement accounting; low-balance failure drills; independently recoverable publisher/guardian; approved treasury and targets. |
| B: bounded off-chain treasury refiller, after A | **8–15 additional days** | One qualified token/route first; exact deltas, slippage/reorg/restart/nonce tests; malicious/restricted-token failures; bootstrap/emergency limits; measured net conversion cost. |
| B with a new treasury/controller contract | **10–20 additional days beyond that service**, plus external review | Restricted selectors/recipients/spend limits, passive receipt compatibility, reentrancy and approval lifecycle tests, deployment/account-code qualification. |
| C: managed sponsorship for operator and publisher | **15–30 days** | Confirmed 4663 provider billing/limits; selected account/EntryPoint code; admission and UserOperation reconciliation; key/module recovery; failure, replay, budget exhaustion and provider outage drills. |
| Per-user escrow variant | **15–30 days**, plus policy decision and external contract review | Explicit fee consent, owner-isolated debits/refunds, relayer cost verification, failure-charge policy, UI receipts and gas bootstrap design. |

A one-token sponsorship or unwrap demo can be much shorter; it does not cover the controls listed above. Reusing a qualified contract wallet or existing provider integration can reduce effort. New treasury custody, multiple illiquid routes or custom paymaster logic increase it.

For A and B, compare the realized 2% against **operator execution + publisher allocation + refill/conversion allocation + mined failures/replacements + provider/operating costs**, then apply the chosen margin. For C, replace the direct payment workflow with measured sponsored-operation cost and provider charges; do not apply 8% to direct EOA gas as if account-validation overhead were zero. Working capital and conversion liquidity remain necessary even if expected long-run revenue exceeds costs. A and B keep the present customer economics; company-funded C can too. Escrow charged on top does not.

## Evidence limits

Local code/policy inspection and public primary-document/source reads support this study. The verified aeWETH source was inspected again for `withdraw`/`withdrawTo`; no unwrap was executed. No current treasury balance, private provider plan, real account permission, current DEX liquidity, router admission, deployed Domino profile, sponsored UserOperation or realized Robinhood gas bill was verified by this work. Therefore the report establishes architectural feasibility and required controls, not a ready-to-run funding configuration. No production source was changed and no runtime/contract test was needed for this documentation-only deliverable.
