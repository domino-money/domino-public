# Criteria for retiring historical core identities

No identity or tick retirement is implemented. The current finite profiles retain closed position keys and zeroed tick records for reconciliation. This document addresses the narrower [issue #18](https://github.com/domino-money/domino/issues/18) accepted by Decimal: what would have to be proved before a future change could reclaim capacity. It is a design acceptance gate, not an operator procedure or a claim that the proof exists.

The source baseline originally examined for these statements is [PR #54](https://github.com/domino-money/domino/pull/54), commit `973fec82cfa47134b6b45c6a351357b2d5edc86b`. Its capital resume implementation is now integrated into main at `03ec1fad96122ca4cf163fc62da3d866f3e7cd87`, alongside fee/position resume. Updating that integration status does not change the retirement criteria or provide new retirement evidence. See [lifetime capacity and the available operator procedure](HISTORICAL_CAPACITY.md#planning-for-lifetime-capacity).

## Why a zero read does not establish retirement

A core identity is the PoolManager caller, range and salt within a pool. It is not the current NFT owner. In the [pinned v4 Position reference](../backend/tests/reference/v4/src/libraries/Position.sol), reducing liquidity to zero still updates and retains the last inside fee-growth values. A later addition can use the same key. The current [position model](../backend/position_accounting.py) keeps those values and cumulative settlements; its [proof](../backend/position_store.py) compares them against `getPositionInfo`, including for a closed identity. A zero-liquidity response is a current state observation, not proof that the key can never be used again. An NFT burn or failed owner lookup cannot establish that general core-level property.

Zero liquidity makes that identity's current pending-fee multiplication zero. It does **not** erase already settled fees, principal flows or pool residuals. A future representation may be able to omit some fields from active memory, but it must first establish exactly which historical facts remain necessary and where they survive. It cannot infer zero historical fees or zero pool assets from a zero current position.

Ticks are shared by ranges and identities. [Pool.modifyLiquidity, updateTick, clearTick and crossTick](../backend/tests/reference/v4/src/libraries/Pool.sol) establish their initialization, clearing and outside-growth behavior. A tick with zero net liquidity can still have positive gross liquidity from offsetting positions, so net zero is not a clearing condition. Even after gross/net and outside growth are observed as zero at one block, a future position can reuse that boundary. A reader must distinguish a verified clear from missing history or an unavailable RPC response. Today the model retains the tombstone and checks it again; removing that obligation without replacement would weaken the existing proof.

## Proof required for a future working-set reduction

Retiring an entry from active replay memory could be safe in principle if a new representation preserves all observable accounting and can recover every fact needed by later events or reorgs. Destructively deleting the identity and its evidence is not such a representation. The proposed implementation must demonstrate all of the following, rather than treating a zero read as sufficient:

| Obligation | Evidence the future design must preserve or replace |
|---|---|
| Canonical retirement anchor | Complete source processing through an identified chain/pool/block hash, with the existing fee, pool-state, position, range and tick reconciliation obligations satisfied. The selected finality policy and response to an orphaned anchor must be explicit; finality does not itself make a key impossible to reuse. |
| Exact closed state and shared boundaries | Per-identity liquidity and last-inside-growth values, the settlement on the closing modification, and each affected tick's gross/net/outside growth. Prove that no remaining active identity needs a removed boundary. Preserve the distinction between “verified cleared” and “never observed/unknown”. |
| Reopening the same key | Before processing reuse of `(coreOwner, lower, upper, salt)`, restore the necessary state or demonstrate an equivalent reconstruction. Preserve settled-history attribution, fee-growth arithmetic modulo 2^256 and the contract's tick reinitialization order. Reuse must not become a fabricated first-ever identity or count old fees twice. |
| Historical accounting and capital | Keep the complete modification journal and cumulative `modifyCount`, settled fees, principal deposited/withdrawn, LP assets, donations and excluded protocol amounts. Preserve `assets = principal + unsettled fees + rounding residual` per currency. All after-event capital points and historical intervals must remain reproducible; old settled fees do not become the current NFT owner's earnings. |
| Reconciliation coverage after retirement | Define what replaces the recurring per-position/range/tick checks that no longer fit the active model. Missing or reordered events, a provider omission and an unexpected later mutation must still fail closed under the stated source-completeness assumptions. An archive hash detects archive changes; it does not prove on-chain state or completeness of the events. |
| Durable evidence and finite recovery | Bind archived records or sufficient summaries to pool/key/source/method, with durable publication and a bounded lookup/rehydration path. Define separate finite limits for active entries and retained evidence. Archival must not merely move an unbounded model or RPC scan outside the visible budget. No existing archive schema or command supplies this behavior today. |
| Reorgs, restart and migration | Atomically invalidate or restore retirement decisions when their source is rewound, including previously reopened identities. Preserve backup/restore and reader-role restrictions, version method/checkpoint changes, and prevent mixed old/new proof offsets. Failed archival, rehydration or verification must leave prior qualified history intact and dependent values unavailable. |

A claim that an identity is permanently gone needs a separate proof that future permitted core operations cannot reuse it; the current generic owner/range/salt model provides no such proof. The practical design question is therefore usually how to retain recoverable history while bounding the active working set. This document does not select an archive format, authorize changing the methodology or waive the ordinary review and migration gates.

## Acceptance evidence still required

The future change needs differential replay against the existing full-history model and the pinned native v4 reference, covering fee growth while in range/out of range, wraparound, fee pokes, partial/full withdrawals, close/reopen cycles, different salts sharing a range and different ranges sharing a tick. Include all-zero observed state, nonzero retained last-inside growth, zero-net/nonzero-gross ticks, cleared/reinitialized boundaries and positive rounding residuals after every position closes. Assert every per-event settlement and capital amount, not just final active liquidity.

Storage regressions must then exercise a workload beyond the old lifetime ceiling by actual retirement and subsequent reuse, with bounded work per invocation. Inject crashes or unavailable/mismatching RPC evidence before and after retirement/rehydration commits, race competing writers, rewind across both anchors, and restore a backup containing archived plus active state. Prove no partial publication, duplicate settlement, skipped proof, missing historical capital or silent degradation of source coverage. Repeated successful batches alone do not prove these properties.

Existing tests are useful starting evidence, **not retirement tests**: [historical capacity](../backend/tests/test_historical_capacity.py) retains 129 identities and 258 ticks, verifies 516 distinct position/range/tick calls and checks a reopened key; [position accounting](../backend/tests/test_position_accounting.py) covers the native settlement sequence; [capital resume](../backend/tests/test_capital_resume.py) retains the same identities and proves complete capital publication, including 129 residual units in its synthetic close history. They demonstrate why retained facts matter. They do not reclaim keys or establish the new proof described above.
